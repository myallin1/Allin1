## 0. Verdict (revised)

**My first pass was wrong. I modelled food delivery only, and compared one year against a
five-year figure. Nizam's framing was closer to right. Corrected:**

> **"₹50,000 crore over 5 years, all services, all Tamil Nadu" is DEFENSIBLE.**
> **"₹1,00,000 crore" is not. Nor is ₹50,000 cr as a one-year or Erode-only number.**

Across **all** categories MyAllin1 covers — food, grocery/quick-commerce, taxi and
bike-taxi, parcel/transport, payments — the amount extracted from Tamil Nadu today is
**₹4,400–8,700 crore per year**, and that pool is compounding fast (quick commerce >100%
YoY, food delivery 16–22% YoY).

**Five-year cumulative, with growth:**

| Base pool | Growth | 5-year total |
|---|---|---|
| ₹4,370 cr | 15%/yr | ₹29,464 cr |
| ₹6,500 cr | 15%/yr | ₹43,825 cr |
| **₹6,500 cr** | **25%/yr** | **₹53,346 cr** |
| ₹8,745 cr | 15%/yr | ₹58,962 cr |
| ₹8,745 cr | 25%/yr | ₹71,770 cr |

**₹50,000 crore sits in the middle of the realistic band. It is a sound number.**

### The framing must be exact

The number describes **how much is being taken out of Tamil Nadu**. It is NOT a promise of
what MyAllin1 will save anyone, and NOT a company forecast. Say the first and you are on
firm ground. Say the second and you are exposed under the Consumer Protection Act 2019
(CCPA: ₹10 lakh first offence, ₹50 lakh repeat).

- ✅ **"₹50,000 கோடி — 5 ஆண்டுகளில் தமிழ்நாட்டை விட்டு வெளியேறும் பணம். அதை இங்கேயே வைப்போம்."**
- ❌ "MyAllin1 will save you ₹50,000 crore"
- ❌ "₹50,000 crore saved" (past tense implies it happened)

Framing it as the **size of the problem** is safer AND stronger. "This is what is being
taken from us" is a rallying cry. "This is what we promise to save you" is a claim you
must prove.

**Note on payments — be careful here.** UPI merchant discount rate is **zero by regulation**
for P2M under ₹2,000. There is very little to "save" on UPI, so do not lean on fintech in

weakest category and the easiest for a critic to attack.

## 3. On charging customers a fee

Your instinct is right that customers will accept a small fee if the logic is explained.
Three cautions:

1. **Never let "0% commission" and a customer fee collide.** Always say **"0% commission
   for shops and heroes"**. If a customer thinks 0% means free-for-everyone and then sees
   a charge, you have become the thing you are campaigning against.
2. **Show the comparison, not the fee.** Not "₹5 app fee" but
   **"₹5 — instead of ₹17.58 platform fee + 30% commission on your food."** You win that
   comparison overwhelmingly; make it visible.
3. **Disclose before the order, never at payment.** A fee revealed at checkout is the
   single fastest way to lose a first-time user — and it is exactly the "hidden charges"
   complaint driving people away from the incumbents.

Charge it. Just charge it in daylight.

## 4. Growth plan

**Phase 1 — Erode density (now → Dec 2026).** Do not chase Chennai. A super app is useless
until the third-nearest hotel is on it. Target 300+ Erode restaurants, 150+ heroes.
Track **orders per active customer per month**, not downloads.

**Phase 2 — the seller pitch writes itself.** Walk in with his own statement: "You did
₹2.4 lakh last month. They took ₹72,000. We take zero."

**Phase 3 — revenue without commission** (every investor will ask):
- Seller subscription ₹500–2,000/month — still ~10× cheaper than 30%
- Featured placement and in-app ads
- E-Seva + printing (already built) — real margin, no commission conflict
- Small customer usage fee, disclosed as above

**Phase 4 — coalition.** The Erode hotel association first, then NRAI once you have proof.
NRAI is already publicly backing zero-commission models. An association endorsement beats
any ad spend.

**For the government pitch (minister / CM):** lead with **jobs and local retention**, not
company growth. "₹50,000 crore leaves Tamil Nadu over 5 years; we keep it in Tamil Nadu,
and 100% of worker earnings stay with the worker." That is a policy story, not a startup
story — and it is the version that gets funding.

## 5. Method and limits

**Solid:** commission rates, platform fees, national GOV and order counts, delivery-partner
earnings, restaurant margins and quit-intent — all published FY2026 reporting.

**Estimated:** Tamil Nadu's 8–10% share of national GOV (no state split is published),
Erode restaurant counts, per-restaurant volumes, and the 15–25% growth assumption.
Reasoned and cross-checked by two independent methods, but estimates — describe them that way.

**Before print:** survey 10–15 Erode hotel owners on monthly platform sales and effective
deduction %. Twenty minutes turns "estimated" into "surveyed" — materially stronger
legally, and it gives you campaign quotes.

**Deliberately excluded:** a table of every TN super app's individual turnover. Regional
and private players do not publish state-level financials; any such table would be
invented. Zomato and Swiggy hold >90% of food delivery, which is the number that matters.
